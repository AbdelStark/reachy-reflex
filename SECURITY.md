# Security and privacy

Reflex's judgments are not a safety controller. Keep motion inside Reachy Mini's SDK and firmware limits and preserve physical stop controls. Motion is opt-in and must stop on stale judgments or lost video; do not bypass that gate when integrating a new sensor or host. The app also holds motion if a Jev answer (including a cached copy) is too old for its source observation, the latest frame is stale, tracked faces move or change identity, or tracking/motion settings change during the request. The 750 ms answer, 500 ms frame, and 8° bearing limits are conservative application choices, not robot-tested safety limits. The 60° camera FOV is uncalibrated, so face-bearing estimates are not safety-grade.

Context changes invalidate in-flight judgments before policy hysteresis or the panel can consume them. A face label recycled after the nine-label table fills is such a change: the old answer is discarded before it can authorize motion for the new face. More than nine detections cannot fit the bank's IDs; the app discards the whole partial scene, stops new judgments, disarms motion, and requires a recovered frame plus explicit re-arm. It does not assume the first nine detections represent the room. Box-only tracking can still swap labels for overlapping or re-entering people; do not treat `p1`–`p9` as verified identities. The Jev client cache is cleared on invalidation and after a late discarded reply; the next accepted policy epoch starts with a fresh, non-stale answer. This does not cancel a network request already sent, stop existing physical motion, or replace the robot's emergency stop.

The browser sends frames only to local MediaPipe inference. The build/dev server fetches a versioned Google model and verifies its SHA-256; the browser loads both model and WebAssembly from the app's origin. MediaPipe 1.0.1 attempts usage-metrics requests to Google after initialization, so face tracking is opt-in and the UI discloses this. The upstream [privacy notice](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe#privacy-notice) says input frames stay on-device and users may need informed consent for metrics. We do not claim the browser is fully offline.

The local relay binds to loopback, requires a long bearer token, allowlists one exact origin, rate-limits calls, caps bodies, and does not log state. It accepts only the reviewed 16-question wire and a bucketed `room_state@1` with session labels, known fields, and at most four 200-character recent-text snippets. Person-dependent attention choices must match the room labels in order. The question digest pin needs review whenever bank wording or options change; it is not a billing budget or a proof that model judgments are correct. This is not a production HTTPS service. Do not place a TypeSafe API key in a browser bundle. Browser transcription is off by default and needs a disclosure checkbox plus Start; it uses the viewer's device microphone and may send audio to a browser-vendor recognition service. Robot-stream transcription is a separate opt-in path: bounded raw audio segments go to an authenticated loopback service with a local model, not to Jev. Obtain consent from people nearby before enabling it. Its energy threshold is not robust VAD, DoA, echo cancellation, or speaker attribution. Both paths send only bounded final text onward to the relay as untrusted data, with speaker identity `unknown`. A final transcript can trigger a model call even with face tracking off; no person target or robot motion is allowed in that state. Energy hints alone do not trigger a text-only call. Stopping clears local text and aborts pending ASR, but an already-started relay request cannot be recalled. Do not expose either service or token to a public network. Report vulnerabilities privately through GitHub without credentials or personal recordings.

The optional local event bridge is disabled unless a separate subscriber token is configured. The browser can publish only typed, text-free policy hints with the model-relay token and the configured origin; WebSocket subscribers authenticate with the distinct read-only token. The bridge caps event size, rate, subscriber count, and pending socket bytes. It has no persistence, replay, consumer acknowledgement, or verified Conversation App hook. A subscriber must treat events as untrusted, expiring hints and must not directly map them to physical stop or motion commands. Disabling publication cannot retract a POST already in flight. Keep the relay bound to `127.0.0.1` and never put either token in a public URL.

The optional speaking-state writer uses a third token that must differ from both event and model tokens. The relay accepts bounded, text-free updates only from a non-browser local client, rejects stale sequences, and expires the assertion after 1.5 seconds. Browser reads require the exact configured origin and model-relay token. A writer is responsible for its own truthfulness and heartbeat: the relay cannot verify that robot audio started, ended, or was physically silenced. If the feed is missing, Reflex does not emit speaking-dependent turn hints and holds a proposed nod; a fresh assertion remains advisory and cannot authorize a TTS stop. Do not expose the writer token, use this as an emergency stop, or interpret relay acceptance as robot evidence.

The optional trace retains at most 1,200 processed ticks in tab memory and has no automatic upload or persistence. Its JSONL projection excludes transcript text, camera frames, and raw audio, but includes approximate face bearings, typed judgments, and motion outcomes. Treat downloads as sensitive; do not publish a real room trace without consent and review. Clearing the trace discards tab-held rows but cannot retract a file already downloaded. Fixture exports are synthetic software tests, not real-world evaluation results.

The committed policy-replay corpus is self-authored synthetic JSONL, not a collection of people or model judgments. The runner has no network, sensor, or robot port. Downloaded `reflex.tick@3` traces can also be replayed locally across recorded policy epochs; they remain potentially sensitive behavioral evidence and must not be treated as independent labels or robot-execution receipts. Older `@2` traces remain replayable but lack the new speaking-known flag. Stale and unavailable judgments, or a gap of at least two seconds between fresh judgments, reset the continuous ignored and no-target timers and partial gaze-switch evidence; otherwise an outage or hidden-tab pause could age old evidence into a droop, idle transition, or gaze switch after recovery. The current pose is held during a short stale interval, and the browser's separate freshness gate still prevents stale judgments from dispatching robot motion. Replay passing does not establish real attention quality or physical safety.
